# Quantum Physics, Step by Step

A friendly, non-specialist guide to quantum physics — built as a
conversation between a curious engineer and Claude, then written up
one step at a time.

## Why this exists

Most explanations of quantum computing start with "a qubit can be 0
and 1 at the same time" and move straight to algorithms. That skips
the part that actually confuses people: *why* any of this follows
from the experiments, and *how* physicists got from firing particles
at a screen to manufacturing a chip.

This guide takes the historical route instead. Each idea arrives
with the problem it was invented to solve.

## How it's organised

Chapters follow the actual chronology, 1900 onward. Every chapter
marks three things explicitly:

- **What survived** — still true today
- **What was discarded** — believed at the time, later shown wrong
- **Why it matters for qubits** — the thread back to computing

The discarded column matters as much as the first. A lot of
confusion comes from carrying around dead ideas — "spin means
something is spinning" being the worst offender.

## Who it's for

Anyone technical but not a physicist. No prior physics assumed.
Mathematics appears only where it earns its place, and is always
explained in words first.
